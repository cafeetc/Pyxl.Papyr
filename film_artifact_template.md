# Film Artifact Analysis Template
## The Portrait (1915) — Frame 1 Splice Tape

*Template v1 — Artifact = detail, Scene = basic context only*

---

### Overview

Frame 1 of *The Portrait* (1915) exhibits a classic single-frame aged pressure-sensitive tape tear repair — physical, base-side adhered, heavily yellowed, hand-torn fibrous edges, adhesive creep/dewetting, transport scuffing, variable translucent-to-opaque density — obscuring portions of the underlying studio scene for one frame only. Clean Frames 2/3 represent the undamaged image.

The artifact presents as an X-shaped radial tape star centered over the actor's upper torso, spanning the full image diagonal. Four arms of varying opacity cross the frame at ~30-45° to horizontal. Transparency zones correspond to underlying image brightness (density_in_frame = D_tape + T_tape × D_print) compounded by physical wear: gate scratches, edge lift, adhesive dry-out.

The shape is a compelling "bird / flying cloth" pareidolia — bilateral asymmetric wings, central body, tail, motion-blur edges, foreground occlusion, single-frame transience — but is definitively a film-surface defect confirmed by: pixel-perfect scene match across three frames, depth-agnostic uniform occlusion, no scene interaction, transmissive bleed-through registered 1:1 with the clean plate, mechanical edge focus with zero optical halation, opacity exceeding scene D-max, frame-locked registration, and aged organic adhesive spectral signature.

The repair is a single-frame "band-aid" star-repair over a radial/perforation-initiated tear at frame center — NOT a frame-line splice — hand-torn, irregular, inconsistent with professional lab practice.

### 1. Artifact Shape, Opacity, Edges, Texture

The defect is a roughly X-shaped radial tape star, centered over the man's upper torso (~52% X, 52% Y), spanning the full image diagonal.

**Arm A — Upper-right:**
- Origin: central mass over man's head/right shoulder.
- Vector: ~30-35° above horizontal, up-right.
- Terminates at top-right corner / frame edge.
- Width: ~18-35% frame height, tapering toward center.
- Opacity: densest arm, ~95-100% opaque. Completely obliterates the back wall portraits it crosses.
- Edges: mechanical, not optical. Upper edge relatively sharp with straight crease-line, micro-fraying, adhesive squeeze-out – hand-torn acetate/cellophane carrier, not razor splice. Lower edge softer, 2-4px feathery halo from edge lift / adhesive dewetting. Scattered specular micro-bubbles along both edges – pressure-sensitive adhesive voids.
- Texture: flat matte, no silver grain, faint longitudinal scuffs, fine craquelure/adhesive crazing. No volumetric shading.

**Arm B — Leftward / horizontal:**
- Origin: central mass, ~48% X, 52% Y.
- Vector: ~5-10° below horizontal, due left.
- Terminates at left frame edge, mid-height.
- Width: ~18-22% frame height, essentially constant-width strap.
- Opacity: variable, 70-95%, print-through dependent.
- Edges: irregular, slightly wavy/torn, micro-fibrils. Small downward nick mid-span over the rolled canvases.
- Texture: matte, prominent longitudinal striations / projector gate wear parallel to arm axis. Mottled adhesive thickness – thicker/blacker over dark underlying areas, thinner/grayer over bright areas.

**Arm C — Lower-left:**
- Origin: central mass lower-left quadrant.
- Vector: ~-35° to -45° (down-left).
- Terminates lower-left quadrant over easel base.
- Width: narrowest arm, ~8-12% frame height at origin, tapering to ~5%.
- Opacity: 65-90%, the most translucent/worn arm overall.
- Edges: highly irregular, feathered, frayed. Dorsal edge sharper; ventral edge diffuse with adhesive ooze.
- Texture: distinctly mottled/cockled – tape shrinkage wrinkling – granular/fibrous look, multiple small air-inclusion/lifting spots giving speckled lighter tone.

**Arm D — Lower-right:**
- Origin: central mass lower-right.
- Vector: ~-25° to -35° (down-right).
- Terminates bottom-right corner, filling it.
- Width: broad wedge, ~22% frame height at origin, expanding to ~38% at frame corner.
- Opacity: 92-100%, second only to Arm A.
- Edges: Upper edge long, slightly concave, mechanically straight/torn. 1-2px adhesive meniscus over tabletop objects, no optical defocus. Lower/right edges frame-cropped.
- Texture: uniformly dense matte black, grain-free, fine craquelure, dust/lint inclusions adhered to tacky surface – sharper than photographic grain.

**Central junction:**
- At ~50-55% X, 48-62% Y, over man's head/neck/upper torso.
- Densest/blackest zone, ~100% opaque, slight physical relief/build-up (multiple tape layers).
- Roughly rhomboid/irregular cruciform, ~18% frame width.
- Overlapping adhesive edges create stepped density – darkest center, radiating arms less dense.
- Small rhomboid air pocket/delamination blister at overlap, faint Newton's-ring-like iridescence – physical lamination on film plane.
- Completely erases the man's face/profile (clearly visible in clean Frames 2/3).

**Carrier vs emulation:**
- Photographic areas show continuous-tone silver grain, halation, sepia coloration.
- Tape areas: zero silver grain, colder/neutral black, matte surface scatter (no glossy emulsion sheen), mechanical edge artifacts (straight cuts, nicks, fibers), uniform density irrespective of focus distance.

### 2. Transparency / Wear Zones

Tape carrier is semi-translucent aged cellulose/cloth with yellowed adhesive. Composite density = tape density + transmitted print density. Bright underlying areas print through; dark areas disappear.

Window edge, canvas cylinder highlight, and a small framed picture ghost through | Transmission + projector gate abrasion polishing the tape thinner over high-contrast edges |
| T2 | Within T1, ~26% X, 50% Y and ~31% X, 52% Y | Gaps between rolled canvases | Two thin (~2-4px) horizontal lighter streaks, ~70% gray | Extreme local tape thinning – linear projector emulsion scratch along film travel, near-clear slit |
| T3 | Arm B, extreme left frame edge, 0-12% X, 45-60% Y | Dark easel canvas | 5-8px feathered gray fringe, 85% → 50% density at edge | Adhesive creep / edge lift – tape delaminated/curling, scanner edge flare. Not print-through (underlying is dark) |

Diagonal light streak matching roll axis | Transmission + adhesive failure/cockling – age shrinkage micro-wrinkles scattering light, partial adhesive dry-out with air micro-bubbles |
| T5 | Tip of Arm C, ~18-22% X, 78-85% Y | Dark floor / easel foot | Soft gray comet tail, ~60% density | Tape lift / end peel – terminal 10-15px lifted off emulsion, accumulated dirt at adhesive edge |

| T7 | Arm A, ~72% X, 38% Y | Portrait sitter's face/collar | Essentially no print-through – 2-3 digital levels lighter at most, scanner noise | Opaque double layer / cloth tape on primary tear. Underlying image is itself dark, no transmitted light. Less projector wear (upper frame sees less gate pressure) |

**Transparency mechanism:** `density_in_frame = D_tape(λ, wear) + T_tape × D_print`. Bright print areas (D_print ~0.2) → composite ~0.8-1.2 OD → medium gray. Dark print areas (D_print ~1.8-2.1) → composite >2.8 OD → maximum black.

### 3. Why It Fools the Eye (Bird / Flying Cloth Read)

**Iconic silhouette – "spread wings":** Arm B left, Arm A up-right form a classic bilateral wingspan, ~85% of frame width, with a central body (the tape junction over the man's torso). Human visual system pareidolically completes a vertebrate/avian body plan.

**Wing taper / aerodynamic planform:** Arm A (right wing) broadens distally, like a primary-feather fan / bat wing, terminating at the frame corner – reads as a foreshortened wing tip close to camera. Arm B (left wing) is long, relatively constant chord with a pointed tip at the left frame edge – reads as the opposite wing extended.

**Tail / trailing cloth:** Arm D lower-right is a broad triangular wedge, exactly the shape/position of a bird's fanned rectrices / a piece of heavy cloth billowing behind, covering the tabletop still life as if draped / in motion.

**Leg / foot / cloth end:** Arm C lower-left is narrower, finger-like, angled down-left – reads as a trailing leg / talon / a torn strip of fabric fluttering.

**Central body occlusion:** The densest tape mass sits precisely where a bird's body / a cloth knot would be – over the man's chest/head – obscuring the man's face, creating the impression of a close foreground object eclipsing the subject.

**Silhouette / backlight logic:** The shape is almost uniformly dark, featureless, with no internal modeling – exactly how a close, fast-moving object silhouetted against a lighter interior would photograph with motion blur / limited depth of field – i.e., a bird flying through the studio, close to the lens, underexposed / motion-smeared to black.

**Motion blur mimicry:** The tape edges are not optically sharp – they are feathered, irregular, with adhesive halos, wear streaks, and mottling – at thumbnail / video speed this reads as motion blur / defocus feathering on wing edges / cloth fringes.

**Dynamic diagonal thrust:** The dominant axis Arm A up-right to Arm C down-left runs ~135°, giving a strong diagonal composition, as if something is diving / swooping from upper right to lower left across the man – a classic "attacking bird" vector.

**Scale / perspective cue:** Because the shape occludes both foreground (right tabletop, ~2 m in-scene) and background (back-wall portraits, ~4-5 m) with equal sharpness/density, the visual system interprets it as extremely close to the camera – i.e., a bird/cloth inches from the lens, hence huge / out of focus, explaining the lack of detail and large angular size.

**Asymmetry naturalism:** Real birds/cloth in flight are rarely perfectly symmetric; the four arms are all different lengths/widths/opacities – Arm A thick and opaque, Arm C thin and mottled – this irregularity paradoxically increases biological/textile plausibility vs a perfect geometric artifact.

**Edge texture misread:** The mechanical torn/fibrous edge of cloth tape, plus longitudinal gate scratches on Arm B, read at low resolution as feather barbs / fabric weave / motion streaks.

**Transparency misread as translucency:** The lighter gray print-through zones T1/T4 over the bright window/rolls – where background shows through the tape – can be misread as a thin/backlit wing membrane / gauze cloth allowing background light to pass – i.e., subsurface scattering in a real wing/fabric, rather than transmission through a semi-transparent repair.

**Temporal singleton / surprise:** In a moving film, a single-frame black flash with a wing-like shape is exactly what a bird flying through at 24 fps would produce – one or two frames of blur – encouraging a "bird strike" interpretation before inspecting adjacent clean frames.

**Contextual priming – "artist's studio with taxidermy?":** The right table holds a sculptural bird / grotesque / organic forms (the fan/peacock object) – a viewer might expect birds/drapery in an artist's atelier, lowering the threshold to accept a flying object.

**Lack of shadow / interaction mis-attributed:** The shape casts no shadow on the man/walls – which is actually impossible for a real object – but at a glance this is attributed to flat studio lighting / motion blur freezing, not to a post-photographic layer.

**Color / tone congruence:** The tape's neutral/cold black is not wildly out of gamut for a shadowed bird/black cloth in a sepia interior – it sits plausibly within the scene's tonal range, especially next to the man's black coat (photographic D-max) – making it less immediately obviously non-photographic to a naive eye.

Together: central body + bilateral asymmetric wings + trailing tail + motion-blur edges + foreground occlusion + single-frame transience = compelling "large black bird / piece of black cloth flying at the camera / attacking the man" illusion.

### 4. Film-Surface Defect Discriminators (vs Diegetic 3D Object)

**A. Pixel-perfect scene identity**
Man's posture, head angle, hand position, coat drape, every painting on the wall, every vase on the table – all pixel-identical between F1/F2/F3 where not obscured. One static camera setup, one moment in time. A real bird/cloth would be a transient absent in clean frames.

**B. Depth violation / uniform occlusion**
Real 3D object occludes foreground OR background with correct parallax/focus. The tape occludes extreme foreground table objects (bottom right), mid-ground man, AND far background wall portraits simultaneously, with no change in focus, blur, or opacity with distance. E.g., right upper arm erases both the close tabletop bust AND the far back wall portrait in one continuous tone.

**C. No scene interaction**
No cast shadow on man/walls, no motion blur consistent with ~1/24s exposure, no fabric folds/anatomical articulation, no specular highlights. Tape intersects hard frame edges arbitrarily – left wing cropped mid-span at left border, right wing at top right corner – typical of torn film repair, atypical of composed in-scene element.

**D. Transmissive bleed-through matches clean plate exactly**
Bright window/paper roll visible through left wing striations is geometrically registered 1:1 with F2's easel. Light passing through semi-transparent stain on emulsion, not semi-transparent bird.

**E. Edge focus – mechanical vs optical**
Tape edges are razor-sharp, sit in the film grain plane, with micro-wrinkles/dust/emulsion scratches that move with the print, not with scene DOF. Underlying image is uniformly softened underneath – aged adhesive effect. Where Arm B crosses the left window niche, the underlying bright window edge (soft photographic edge with ~4-6px halation in F2) is abruptly terminated at the tape boundary – physical opaque interposer, not optical obstruction.

No optical diffusion halation, no dye-cloud bleed, no registration fringing at tape/image boundaries.

**F. Opacity exceeds scene blacks**
Tape D-max is darker than man's coat or darkest painting in F2/F3 – impossible for a photographed object under same exposure.

Quantitative spot check: Arm B over photographic black (~8,8,8 RGB in F2) = RGB 12,12,14 in F1. Same arm 80px right over window white (~210,195,170) = RGB 95,88,78. An 80+ level lift tracking the print – transmissive overlay.

**G. Registration – frame-locked, not scene-locked**
Tape's left edge sheared exactly at left film frame edge; right wing exits top right corner; lower tail exits bottom right. Locked to film rectangle, not set geometry.

**H. Spectral signature**
Broadband yellow-brown absorption, no dichroic color fringing – aged organic adhesive, not digital overlay. Scan interlacing/compression macroblocking orthogonal to tape edges – rules out digital codec error.

**I. Temporal behavior**
Artifact present for exactly one frame in sequence. No frame-to-frame registration shift, no jump cut, no density/color timing step between F1 (artifact) and F2 (clean) – underlying image pixel-registered, confirming tape exists only on the source element used for the F1 scan.

**J. No parallax / motion blur**
No stereoscopic parallax, no motion blur on tape edges – tape is static relative to film grain for duration of single exposed frame. Adhered to film, not a lens/gate hair.

### 5. Physical Artifact Behavior

**5A Adhesive behavior**
Pressure-sensitive adhesive, extensively degraded. Classic age failure modes:
- Yellowing/ambering concentrated at edges and in pooled channels
- Adhesive creep / cold flow beyond carrier margins → translucent meniscus halo
- Reticulation / alligatoring – fine mottled granularity within tape body, especially lower-left translucent limb
- Micro-bubble / dewetting voids along edges and at overlap – loss of tack, base shrinkage
- No cement solvent ring, no splice cement staining – **not a cement splice**

**5B Wear patterns**
- Longitudinal micro-scratching / scuffing parallel to tape long axis – projector gate / transport wear on raised tape bump
- Emulsion lifting / pick-off along upper-right limb crease – tape has pulled emulsion when previously lifted
- Edge curl / dog-earing at right margin where tape exits frame – triangular lift catching highlight, confirming physical thickness

**5C Aging / deterioration**
- Uniform sepia / tobacco browning, denser at edges and overlap adhesive mass
- Embrittlement consistent with 30-70 year old cellophane / cellulose acetate "Scotch" type mending tape
- Shrinkage pull – bowstring edge profile, minor cockling / geometric distortion of background picture frames visible under lower-left limb

**5D Splice / junction morphology**
- **NOT a frame-line splice** – single-frame "band-aid" tear repair
- Diagonal crossing ~30-45° to horizontal frame line
- Hand-torn irregular terminations at all four frame edges
- Inconsistent with professional lab tape splice (would be transverse, full-width, razor-cut, at frame line)
- X-pattern = star-repair over radial / perforation-initiated tear at frame center, stabilized from both sides with overlapping strips
- No frame-to-frame registration shift, no jump cut, no density/color timing step – pixel-registered underlying image
- Artifact present for exactly **one frame**

**5E Image density interaction**
- Occlusion is multiplicative with scene density
- Bright areas (window/wall left, light portrait frame center) – tape visible as warm brown density veil, proving it's in optical path above the image, not camera-original obstruction
- Dark areas (man's coat, large dark canvas right) – tape merges with D-max, visible only by edge relief
- Shadow detail under translucent lower limb: compressed but recoverable, no dye destruction – neutral-density absorptive filter in contact
- No halation, no infectious development, no printer light spill

**5F Other artifact characteristics**
- Non-diegetic, non-repeating – absent in F2/F3, where man, bric-à-brac, and all background paintings are fully legible
- No perforation damage, cue marks, or frame line intrusion (in crop)
- No stereoscopic parallax, no motion blur on tape edges – static relative to film grain
- Scan interlacing / compression macroblocking orthogonal to tape edges – rules out digital codec error
- Spectral: broadband yellow-brown absorption, no dichroic fringing – aged organic adhesive, not digital overlay
